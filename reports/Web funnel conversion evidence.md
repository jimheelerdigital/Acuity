# Fix the gates before adding the demo

The best-documented web-to-app funnels (Noom, Calm, Flo, Liven, Cal AI) share one shape: a one-tap opening question, a long quiz of roughly 20–60 screens that makes the user feel understood, a "building your plan" loader, an **email-only** gate framed as "see my results", a personalised result that mirrors the user's answers, and then a paywall with trust signals under the button. The account and password come after payment or inside the app. Ripple's current data says its losses happen at the entry and at the gates, not in the quiz. Almost everyone who taps screen 1 reaches account creation. Half of them are lost at a password step, which is about 3x the 18% abandonment Baymard attributes to forced account creation. The paywall-to-checkout rate is already in a normal range. For /start-test, the evidence supports three things: first, prove whether the 85–95% screen-1 bounce is real or inflated by Meta prefetch; second, replace account creation with an email-only gate before the paywall and move the password to after payment; third, rebuild the paywall around annual-plus-monthly pricing, an Apple Pay express button, and a Blinkist-style trial-reminder timeline. The "real demo before signup" idea draws on some sound mechanisms (Duolingo's delayed signup, the IKEA effect, the labor illusion). But no published test shows that real AI output before a paywall beats simulated personalisation. Microphone capture is unreliable in the Facebook and Instagram in-app browsers where this traffic lands. So the demo belongs in the funnel as an optional, type-first A/B arm, not as the core design. One caveat covers every Ripple number here: ~140 visitors in one day is too little data to rank problems with confidence. The benchmarks below should set the targets, and a week or more of clean data should confirm which gate is worst.

## Ripple loses visitors at three gates, and the quiz is not one of them

Put the current numbers through the funnel. From ~140 sessions, 5–15% tap screen 1, which is 7–21 people. Nearly all of them reach account creation, and about half get past it. That leaves **roughly 2.5–7.5% of sessions reaching the paywall, against a vendor benchmark of about 13%** ([FunnelFox State of Web2App 2026](https://funnelfox.com/state-of-web2app/)). Half of paywall viewers start Stripe checkout, so roughly 1–4% of sessions start checkout. FunnelFox reports about **3.0% session-to-purchase for web funnels versus 1.5% for app funnels**. It also says "the biggest drop happens before the paywall" ([FunnelFox](https://funnelfox.com/state-of-web2app/)). Ripple matches that pattern, but its pre-paywall losses are concentrated in two places, not spread across the quiz. The vendor figure for first-screen loss is "over 50% of users drop off after the first screen, before answering a single question" ([web2wave](https://www.web2wave.com/post/quiz-builder); [FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/creative-testing-for-web2app/)). Ripple's 85–95% is much worse than that, which is exactly why measurement should be checked first (see below).

The account step is the clearest fixable leak. Across 50 studies, Baymard finds that among checkout abandoners, **18% leave because the site wanted them to create an account**. Another 17% leave because checkout was too long ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). A ~50% drop at one account-plus-password screen is far outside that range. It is also the step the top funnels avoid. Noom takes an email right before results and puts payment, then account confirmation, then app login at the very end ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)). Flo creates the account after the quiz, framed as protecting progress already made ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-funnel-patterns-2026-part-2/)). Calm is the exception: it requires an account before the trial ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-funnel-examples)). Calm can afford that with brand trust Ripple does not yet have.

Ripple's paywall-to-checkout-start rate of ~50% is healthy. Cal AI, the most instrumented app in the evidence, reports that **57% of paywall viewers start a transaction and 63% of those complete it** ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/case-studies/cal-ai)). That puts Ripple roughly on par at the paywall. The unmeasured risk sits after the checkout button. FunnelFox reports that **30–50% of initiated web payments fail on the first attempt**, and retries recover about half of them ([FunnelFox](https://funnelfox.com/state-of-web2app/)). Ripple should log checkout-started, checkout-succeeded and first-attempt failure as separate events.

## Diagnose the screen-1 bounce before redesigning it

For some mobile News Feed ads, Meta loads the landing page before anyone clicks. It "prefetches the initial HTML page when the story first appears on a person's screen" if its model predicts a click. This inflates traffic and click counts in third-party measurement ([ThriveTracker](https://thrivetracker.com/blog/filtering-out-facebooks-pre-fetch-prefetching-clicks-inflating-your-stats)). Adobe Analytics users report that prefetch "greatly inflate[s] visits and bounce rates with artificial visits" ([Adobe community](https://experienceleaguecommunities.adobe.com/adobe-analytics-3/prefetching-online-ad-email-links-inflating-website-visits-adobe-analytics-89361)). Separately, agencies treat a gap of more than 15% between Meta link clicks and Landing Page Views as normal, because a click counts even when the user leaves before the page loads ([Nudge](https://giveitanudge.com/link-clicks-vs-landing-page-views-big-discrepancy/)). No source quantifies what share of ad "visits" are prefetches. So the problem cannot be estimated from outside. It has to be measured.

The diagnosis takes four numbers for the same day and campaign: Meta link clicks, Meta Landing Page Views, a first-party "screen 1 rendered" event fired client-side after the page becomes interactive, and first-answer taps. Server-side or page-load counts should exclude requests carrying prefetch headers (`Sec-Purpose: prefetch` or `X-Purpose: preview` are the usual browser conventions, but no source confirms which one Meta sends, so check the raw request logs). Read the result like this. If rendered views are far below server "visits", the denominator is inflated and the true bounce is lower. If link clicks are far above LPVs, the problem is load speed in the in-app browser. Generic web data shows conversion falling from **2.3% at a 1-second load to 1.5% at 5 seconds** ([OuterBox](https://www.outerboxdesign.com/articles/cro/page-speed-conversion-statistics/)). If LPVs are healthy but taps are still under ~40% of rendered views, the problem is design or ad-to-page mismatch. Only that last case justifies a first-screen redesign. A session-replay sample of 20–30 visits will show whether answer buttons sit below the fold or render late in the in-app browser.

If design is the problem, the evidence points to a one-tap, single-select question that matches the ad's promise. Calm opens with "gentle self-recognition and safety", a question about how often a problem happens, and reassurance. Noom opens with a goal choice ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-funnel-patterns-2026-part-2/); [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-funnel-examples)). BetterMe runs separate funnels per segment (men, women, older users) rather than one generic entry ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web-funnels-insights-and-trends/)). That supports a distinct BWK entry screen for men, not a gender picker on a shared page. Visible progress bars reportedly lift quiz completion by 12–18% ([GrowthLens](https://www.growthlens.io/blog/quiz-funnel-completion-rate-optimization)). That is a vendor figure with no stated method, but it is a cheap change.

## The funnel the evidence supports for /start-test

Health and wellbeing funnels are long on purpose. Calm and Flo each run about 40 screens, Liven (the closest self-discovery analogue) runs 42 steps with 26 questions, and Noom grew from 26 screens in 2020 to 113 ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-funnel-patterns-2026-part-2/); [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)). FunnelFox calls **30–60 screens "acceptable in health and vulnerability-driven categories"**. No named app has published a controlled length test, so length is practice rather than proof. Ripple's data does make lengthening low-risk: almost no one drops mid-quiz once they tap in. The recommendation is **about 20–28 screens**. That is long enough to hold the mirror-back and commitment moments the category leaders use, and short enough to fit Ripple's single-product scope. Rotate formats (single-select, multi-select, sliders between opposing statements, "prefer not to say") as Noom and Liven do. Avoid free text, except in the optional demo.

The email gate is the change most likely to matter. Top funnels put it just before results because "the overwhelming majority of users who leave their email address never become subscribers on that session". Paywall-abandon sequences then recover an estimated 5–20% of revenue with a cadence of 1–2 hours, 24 hours, 48–72 hours and day 5–7 ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/retargeting-emails-in-web2app/)). Moving the password after payment has a known cost that must be managed: **40–50% of web payers never register in the app** ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-quizzes-as-profit-engine/)). Create the account automatically from the checkout email, send a magic link immediately, and deep-link the app download straight into a signed-in first session.

Social proof should appear three times: one honest trust line near the start, one real review mid-quiz, and ratings, reviews and an FAQ under the paywall button. Flo and Runna both put the trust block after the plans and CTA ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-funnel-patterns-2026-part-2/)). Reviews carry weight: five reviews raise purchase likelihood by 270% over none, more for higher-priced items, with returns diminishing after about five and purchase likelihood peaking at average ratings of 4.0–4.7 ([Spiegel Research Center](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/)). A handful of real, specific reviews is enough. Invented user counters of the "3,627,436 people" kind are not an option for Ripple.

### The paywall: transparency over urgency

The paywall evidence splits into two camps. Aggressive funnels (Liven, Headway, BetterMe, Fastic) use three plans, countdown timers, "reserved discounts" and exit offers. Softer ones (Calm in one variant, Flo, Runna, Cal AI) use fewer options and reminder promises ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/web2app-funnel-patterns-2026/)). Timers and personalised coupons show about 15% lifts in vendor client tests ([Botsi](https://www.botsi.com/blog/web2app-paywall-conversion)). But practitioners report that discounted users churn faster ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/optimize-app-paywall-higher-conversion/)). A published review of Liven is titled "When well-being becomes a stressful funnel" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@tsholok/liven-review-when-well-being-becomes-a-stressful-funnel-230eae1b0fb1)). For women in their 40s buying a reflection product, the best-evidenced lever is the opposite of urgency. Blinkist's trial paywall showed a timeline (today, reminder two days before the trial ends, charge date) and a visible exit. It **lifted trial signups 23% and cut complaints 55%** ([Growth.Design](https://growth.design/case-studies/trial-paywall-challenge)). Mimo's "honest trial" paywall more than doubled trial opt-in ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/optimize-funnel-metrics-mimo)).

The recommended paywall is two plans with annual preselected. Show the annual as **$7.50/mo, billed $89.99/yr** next to $9.99 monthly. Adding a discounted annual next to monthly raised trial conversion 22% in one Superwall case, though that case used a deeper 60% discount ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/3-proven-paywall-and-pricing-experiments-to-boost-indie-app-revenue)). Add the Blinkist timeline and a "Start my 7-day free trial" CTA. The CTA wording matters a little: "Get my plan"-style wording lifted conversion up to 5% ([Botsi](https://www.botsi.com/blog/web2app-paywall-conversion)). Put an **Apple Pay / Google Pay express button above the card form**. Stripe's holdback test found Apple Pay raised eligible conversion 22.3%, and showing it early via Express Checkout roughly doubled its effect ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/testing-the-conversion-impact-of-50-plus-global-payment-methods)). Apple Pay is about 60% of web2app transactions ([FunnelFox](https://funnelfox.com/state-of-web2app/)). One point needs a device check: no source confirms how reliably Apple Pay appears inside the Facebook and Instagram in-app browsers. The pricing itself is sound. $9.99 is exactly the Health & Fitness monthly median, and **higher-priced apps convert downloads to paid at 2.8% vs 1.4% for low-priced ones** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Keep the 7-day trial: 5–9 day trials convert at a 37.4% median, against 25.5% for trials of 4 days or less. One contrary data point deserves a note without acting on it yet. FunnelFox reports no-trial offers make 56.8% of web2app revenue and "consistently outperform" trial flows ([FunnelFox](https://funnelfox.com/state-of-web2app-2026)). That is a later test, once the gates are fixed.

## The live demo is plausible but unproven, and voice is the weak link

The idea behind the proposed "talk or type, then see your own task list before account creation" step has real support. Duolingo moved signup behind the first lesson and got **about a 20% increase in DAUs**, plus another 8.2% from soft walls ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker/)). The IKEA effect shows people value what they made themselves ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740811000829)). The labor illusion shows that visible work raises perceived value ([HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=40158)). RevenueCat's teardown of Noom complains that "after all of this, I still don't know what the app experience actually looks like" ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)). A user seeing her own tangled thoughts sorted into a list is a more honest "result" than a projected chart, and it fits the mirror positioning.

Three things are unproven, and one is a known risk. First, the Duolingo result measured engagement in a freemium app, not paid conversion in a card-required trial funnel. Second, **no public A/B test compares real AI output with simulated personalisation before a paywall** for any consumer app. The best-known AI subscription funnel, Cal AI, converts on ~32 screens of quiz and projected plan. It gates the real scan behind the paywall and credits its revenue growth to 61 onboarding-paywall experiments ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/case-studies/cal-ai)). Third, the IKEA effect holds only "when labor results in successful completion of tasks" ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-ce76-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)). A stalled recording or a weak extraction should do worse than no demo at all. The known risk is the microphone. Developers report `getUserMedia` audio failing in iOS in-app browsers since iOS 15.1 with no Apple fix ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/699479)), and mic access blocked in the Instagram and Messenger in-app browsers ([Bubble forum](https://forum.bubble.io/t/defeated-w-microphone-access-inappbrowser-fb-messenger-instagram-limits-access/104984)). Even on desktop Chrome, only 29.8% allow a permission prompt that follows a user interaction ([CHI 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642252)). The audience adds a trust factor. Only 17% of US women expect AI to affect them positively, against 29% of men ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/the-gender-gap-in-ai/)). Those forum reports date from 2020–2023, and a hands-on test on current iOS and Android builds of both apps should come before any build decision.

That points to a specific test design. The demo should be **optional, placed mid-quiz after commitment is built** (around screen 14), not at screen 1. An effortful first step would almost certainly make the entry bounce worse. It should default to a typed box with a microphone button, include one line on what happens to the words, and offer a "show me an example instead" skip that falls back to the static preview. Run it as a 50/50 split against the same funnel with the static sample only. Log four things: microphone permission success by user agent, demo start and completion rates, extraction latency, and the downstream funnel. The sample-size arithmetic matters at Ripple's traffic. Detecting a 30% relative lift in session-to-trial from a ~3% baseline takes roughly 6,400 sessions per arm (standard two-proportion calculation, 80% power), about three months at 140 sessions a day. Use email-gate submission and paywall-view-to-trial as the early read (detectable in 2–3 weeks at higher base rates), and keep trial start as the deciding metric. Run the demo test **after** the email-gate and paywall changes ship. Otherwise a 50% account-step leak will drown any demo effect.

Two warnings apply to this table. Most web2app figures come from FunnelFox, RevenueCat and Superwall, which sell funnel tooling and chose which customers to feature. None of the big datasets reports web card-trial conversion separately from in-app. And trial-to-paid below ~30% would point to first-charge failures or low-intent traffic, not the funnel. Redux Payments warns, on unaudited internal data, that the first post-trial web charge is the weakest link in a card trial ([Redux Payments](https://www.reduxpayments.com/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate)). Track "trial ended → charge attempted → charge succeeded" on its own.

## Conclusion

Several changes will likely work. Measuring screen 1 correctly will reveal how much of the bounce is real. Replacing account creation with an email-only "see my results" gate should recover much of the ~50% loss at that step and gives the funnel a retargetable lead. A mirror-style result screen, a transparent loader, an Apple Pay express button, and a Blinkist-style trial timeline all have the strongest evidence of any paywall change. A few things will likely not work or are not worth it. A voice-only demo will fail for a large share of in-app-browser traffic. Timers, fake discounts and invented user counts are only proven on audiences unlike Ripple's, and they carry brand risk. Weekly plans retain about 3% at 12 months on web. Shortening the quiz would target a problem Ripple does not have. Moving the demo to screen 1 would make the worst leak bigger.

The broader point: the published evidence is about gates and paywalls, not about the ideas that feel most new. Cal AI grew through 61 paywall experiments, not a clever demo. Ripple's own data already shows where the money leaks. The real-demo idea is worth one clean A/B test. At current traffic, though, it can only be read properly once the gates stop losing half the people who reach them. And whatever it shows will be the first public evidence on the question, because no competitor has published any.
