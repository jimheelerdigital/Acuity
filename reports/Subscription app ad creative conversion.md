# Show the insight, not the mood

For subscription apps like Ripple, the Meta creatives with the best evidence are **cheap, native-looking statics that say literally what the product does and show one concrete result**. That means text walls, notes-app and letter formats, social-post and review mockups, infographics and product demos. Polished, AI-looking mood imagery is the weakest bet. Across $1.29B of Meta spend, text-only ads hit at **11.6%** against **6.9% for high-production ads**, and demos are the number-two visual format. The category's longest-running ads are rarely clever: one plain benefit or proof line, reused under many rotating visuals for 100 to 390+ days. Ripple's own winners ("Two years of noticing", "Same patterns. Different year.") fit that pattern exactly. That tells the pipeline where to go: more literal input-to-output and pattern-reveal concepts, fewer AI photographs, and much more variety in angle and format rather than in styling. At ~$100/day the limit is not how many ads can be produced but how much conversion volume there is to judge them. A lane gets perhaps 4–9 trials a week, so each ad has to be judged on attention and funnel signals first, and on cost per trial only once it has earned spend. Policy is the other hard boundary. Meta's Personal Attributes rule forbids implying the reader has any health or mental-health condition, *including as a question*. It also forbids implying the reader's age, so copy for the 40–50 lane has to name the life situation, never the demographic. The final section turns all of this into a weekly spec: a 10-ad format mix per lane, hook templates written in Ripple's voice, copy rules, visual direction, a rubric for the founder's two picks, kill/scale thresholds and a measurement plan. It also marks which recommendations need video.

## Literal, native-looking statics outperform polished mood work

No published dataset reports format performance for subscription apps measured on trials. The large datasets are dominated by e-commerce (Motion), app-install gaming and social apps (AppsFlyer, Liftoff), or ad volume without performance (FunnelFox). Taken together, though, they point the same way. Motion's 2026 benchmark covered **$1.29B of Meta spend and 578,750 creatives**. A "winner" there is an ad reaching 10x its account's median spend, and only about 5% qualify. The strongest visual formats were **offer-first banners (8.6% hit rate), demos (8.1%) and testimonials (6.5%)**. The "ugly ad" family (letter, unconventional text placement, post-it) had outsized spend-use ratios ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)). Motion itself warns that the offer-banner lead is partly an artifact of Black Friday. Split by production style, the ranking is clearer: **text-only 11.60%, product image plus text 8.75%, UGC 7.56%, high production 6.87%, animation 4.57%** ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/meta-ads-in-2026-how-many-creatives-do-you-actually-need-to-launch/)). In Health & Wellness, the formats taking the most spend relative to their share of ads were **social post mockup, letter**, case study, founder and transformation ([Motion by vertical](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)).

App-specific practitioners agree. Marcus Burke, the best-known Meta consultant for subscription apps, singles out **"text wall" ads**: a complete story written out over a plain background, which "looks like organic content". He also recommends **infographics** for habit trackers because they deliver value inside the ad. He calls talking-head UGC oversaturated ([RevenueCat / Burke](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/meta-ads-in-2025-tips-for-apps)). AppsFlyer's 1.1M-variation report found **tutorial-style UGC and app reviews beat classic testimonials by 45% on install rate and 17% on day-7 retention**, in Social apps ([AppsFlyer](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250429844196/en/AppsFlyers-2025-Creative-Report-Reveals-How-AI-and-Emotion-Drive-Creative-Performance)). The mechanism is the same throughout. Ads that show what the product does, or read like something a real person posted, beat ads that look designed.

Letterly is the closest comparable business: voice in, text out, sold through a web quiz funnel. Its live library holds **338 active ads, 288 of them static images**. Its highest-impression ads carry the plainest possible copy: "Turn your thoughts into well-written text," "Record VOICE and turn it into perfectly written TEXT," and a "Perfect for:" emoji list of use cases ([Letterly Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=108316755707654)). The copy is almost clichéd, and it scales anyway. A clear transformation matters more than a well-crafted line, especially in a category where "voice journal" or "AI life optimizer" does not explain itself. Practitioners give the same diagnosis for app funnels that leak: a leading reason installs fail to become trials is that "the ad creative promised one thing and the app delivered another" ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/why-subscription-app-get-installs-no-paid-subscribers)).

Static versus video depends on placement, and placement decides who sees the ad. Meta's own split tests found **9:16 video with audio got 34.5% lower cost per result than images**. Those were Reels-only campaigns in e-commerce, which is a structural advantage for video ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-vertical-video-formats-452902)). For subscription apps, Burke says statics naturally go to Facebook Feed. Feed reaches older, more qualified buyers: a higher initial cost per trial, but **the best trial-to-paid rates**. Instagram Reels is younger and lower-intent. In Blinkist's web funnel, about **80% of impressions landed on Facebook Feed** ([RevenueCat / Burke](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/meta-ads-in-2025-tips-for-apps)). For Ripple's women-40–50 lane, a static-heavy mix is not a compromise. It is where that audience is. BetterMe, which ships 500–1,000 creatives per funnel per month, keeps "a meaningful share" of statics in its mental-health flows specifically ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/betterme-web2app-ads-analysis/)). The BWK lane is different: young men sit mostly on Reels, which is the strongest argument for adding video there first.

AI imagery is the pipeline's main visual risk. NIQ's neuro study (2,000+ participants, ~150 on EEG) found consumers **spotted most AI-generated ads and rated them more "annoying, boring, confusing"**, with weaker memory activation even for high-quality ones, plus a negative halo on the brand ([NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/)). Published "AI UGC versus real UGC" performance numbers come from vendors and contradict each other ([inBeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/ai-ugc-ads-vs-real-ugc); [dansugc](https://dansugc.com/blog/ai-ugc-vs-real-human-ugc-converts-better-2026)). Practitioners still use AI heavily, but as faceless B-roll and backgrounds, not as the human subject ([RevenueCat / Burke](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/meta-ads-in-2025-tips-for-apps)). For a trust-dependent buyer in her 40s, an obviously synthetic woman staring out of a window is the weakest version of the ad. Ripple's current pattern (winners are concrete insight ads, generic moody lines lose) is consistent with that. It is not a coincidence.

## Category leaders keep one plain line and rotate everything under it

The Meta Ad Library has no spend data, so how long an ad runs is the best available sign of profit. On that measure the pattern is stark. The longest-running ads in the category are one-line benefit or proof claims, copied across dozens of visual variants. BetterMe's "28 Days Challenge To Lose Weight At Home" has run **385–392 days** across 8+ videos with identical copy ([BetterMe Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=116700222203350)). Speechify's "Need to hear it to believe it?" has run **395 days** ([Speechify Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=248986568895243)). Calm's "iPhone App of the Year" has run **303** ([Calm Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=328627523855071)). Noom's "What's stopping you?" has run **293** ([Noom Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=112936925459222)). Headway's "Turn self-development into laid-back and easy leisure" has run **220** as an image plus two videos sending traffic to a web quiz ([Headway Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=250289965916061)). The Fabulous has run a single copy block ("Morning can be your favorite part of the day!… 40,000,000+ members") under most of its creatives for **141–202 days** ([Fabulous Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=290983137688447)). In every case **the winning unit is the angle and its line, while the visual assets rotate underneath it**, get localized, and are relaunched as new ad IDs.

Liven documents the discovery process behind that pattern. It ships 1,000+ creatives a month and moved through a sequence of angles: procrastinators, then childhood trauma, then dopamine detox, then cortisol detox, then gender-specific variants. Each new angle was **tested as a text-heavy image inside the existing control copy**, then scaled into many creatives. Its formats included "Apple Notes-style static images, charts and graphs… text-heavy static images" ([Funnel of the Week](https://blog.funneloftheweek.com/p/fb-ad-creative-fatigue-angle-iteration-strategy-liven-app-uses-completely-defeat)). Opal works the same way at small scale. Its main script ("You keep telling yourself 'just five more minutes.' It's never five.") has run for 104 days and been relaunched monthly. A student variant swaps only the opening scene: "the exam is thursday. you've reread the same page four times between tiktoks" ([Opal Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=111491020374567)). The implication for Ripple is direct. Once "Same patterns. Different year." wins, the job is to put new visuals, new formats and new opening scenes around that line. Replacing it with a new line every week throws the winner away.

The web-quiz advertisers also share a destination grammar. Noom, Liven, Headway, Letterly and Fabulous send all or nearly all traffic to a web quiz with the CTA "Learn more". Their copy sells the quiz result more than the app: "Take this FREE 5min quiz," Rise's "find out your number. Most people are shocked by theirs," Liven's "Easy to Follow Anti-Burnout Plan" ([Rise Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=1023599077769643); [Liven Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=103537499312980)). **None of the long-running wellness or productivity ads pulled mention price.** Install-only ads do mention the free trial (Calm's "Prueba 7 días gratis", Speechify's "Try for FREE!"). Web-quiz ads leave the offer to the funnel.

The AI-journal niche is small and leaves a gap open. Rosebud runs 16 active ads. Its longest-running concept is a first-person testimonial about bringing the **weekly summary** to therapy, and it is the only competitor that clearly pitches "surfacing patterns and insights you'd miss on your own" ([Rosebud Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=135252226332167)). Mindsera copies Headway's "15 Minutes a Day Can Change Everything" template almost word for word ([Mindsera Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=109458634048633)). Voicenotes' meeting-note ads ran 85–88 days, while its journaling-framed static died in 2–4 days ([Voicenotes Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=261987383666277)). That suggests "journaling" alone is a weak pitch for a voice app. What works is a concrete output: text, tasks, a summary. Nobody in the sample runs **"talk it out, then see your to-do list, your patterns and your weekly report"** at overloaded midlife women. That is Ripple's lane to own, and its winners already show it works. Several competitor hooks break Ripple's rules as written ("brain dump", "10 minutes a day", "75% of users feel better within 7 days"), so they have to be translated rather than copied.

## Winning copy stacks a specific scene, a literal mechanism and one true proof

In the long-running app ads, the most common copy structure is a compressed stack. **One line names a specific, slightly embarrassing moment in the reader's own inner voice. One line says literally what the product does. One line of proof follows. An outcome-led headline of 3–7 words sits under the creative.** Opal's winner is the clearest example: confession ("It's never five"), then mechanism ("Opal blocks the apps pulling you in so you actually stop"), then stat, then feature list, then headline "Get your hours back" ([Opal Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&view_all_page_id=111491020374567)). BetterMe's mental-health line uses hyper-specific scene callouts: "Ever turn a simple 'no' into a five-minute explanation?" ([Atria](https://www.tryatria.com/ads/meta/betterme-mental-health-ads)). Adapty's app guidance compresses this to "start with the pain, the emotion, the moment" and bring in the app afterwards ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/first-10k-meta-ads-subscription-apps/)). No controlled study ranks hook archetypes for apps. This is observation and practitioner consensus, not proof. But it matches Ripple's own evidence: the specific beats the atmospheric.

Meta shows about **125 characters of primary text** before "See more" on mobile ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/recommended-ad-copy-length/)). So the scene line and the mechanism line both need to fit in the visible part. Opal runs a short script (~330 characters) and a long advertorial (~1,200 characters) at the same time. The long one goes to its web funnel and stacks a named testimonial, a percentage stat, a member count and press quotes. Practitioners suggest that cold traffic tolerates longer copy, and that length should be tested only after a hook has won ([roaspig](https://roaspig.com/blog/facebook-ad-copy-length/)).

The two lanes need different evidence and different proof. For women 40–55, Burke's rule is that "creative is targeting": copy about older-life problems pulls older responders, and statics pull delivery toward Facebook Feed, where those responders are ([Appfigures / Burke](https://appfigures.com/resources/videos/mastering-meta-ads-for-apps-w-marcus-burke)). **Press and credibility persuade older users; influencer shoutouts persuade younger ones** ([Burke](https://marcuscnslt.substack.com/p/audience-aware-funnel-design)). The tone risk is real. In IPA research, only **7% of women aged 49–64** felt brands cater to them adequately, and about two-fifths called ads aimed at their life stage unrelatable and outdated ([Advertising Week](https://advertisingweek.com/heath-wellness-brands-are-missing-out-on-the-menopause-moment/)). This audience wants its experience validated in plain language, without medical framing and without the stock "juggling mom" image ([Landing Partners](https://www.landing.partners/blog/womens-health-supplement-marketing-hormonal-balance-perimenopause)). The mental-load frame is well grounded: P&G's campaign reported **71% of women experience mental load against 12% of men**, and dramatized it by comparing to-do lists ([Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/mental-load-does-it-sound-familiar)). Tools in the space describe their value as making invisible tasks visible ([RemindHer](https://remindher.app/best-app-for-mental-load/)). Ripple does that literally: what you said becomes a list.

For men 18–34, the live vocabulary is identity statements ("I don't chase, I attract"), reclaiming time from the phone ("Get your hours back"), time-boxed challenges ("Disappear for one month and come back") and precision ([Headway teardown](https://intelligentartifice.net/episode/deconstructing-headways-50000-ad-engine-identity-urgency-scale/); [Rise on Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/rise-science)). This audience starts trials easily and converts to paid poorly. Burke warns that entertainment-style creative for young men undermines a paid subscription ([Burke](https://marcuscnslt.substack.com/p/audience-aware-funnel-design)). BWK ads should therefore sell the scoreboard and the accountability, not the vibe, and should be judged on trial-to-paid rather than on trial cost. Several men's-space hooks (testosterone, dopamine, cortisol) are health claims Ripple cannot make.

Social proof has to be real, and Ripple's supply of it is thin. Long-running competitors lean on stars, "#1", member counts and press. Ripple has no press and should not invent counts. So the proof slot should hold **verbatim reviews or user quotes with permission**, or a literal product fact (it scores 6 areas, it sends a weekly report). A native-looking review screenshot "looks captured, not designed" ([addogs.ai](https://www.addogs.ai/blog/testimonial-ad-examples)). That makes a real review in a Notes or Facebook-post frame one of the strongest statics available.

## At $100 a day, diversity of concept beats volume of ads

Andromeda is Meta's retrieval engine. It narrows "tens of millions of ads to the few thousand" considered for each impression ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)). A second claim, that near-duplicate ads are grouped under a single "Entity ID" and compete with each other, comes from practitioners, not from Meta's documentation ([adsuploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-andromeda)). A reported "similarity score" threshold is unverified. The practical advice is the same in every source anyway: **reskins of one idea behave like one ad, while different angles, formats and personas reach different people** ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)). Ripple's recent change giving "each weekly ad its own visual style" helps only if the angle and format change too. Ten ads that say the same thing in ten color schemes count as roughly one test.

The budget sets the pace. Motion finds accounts under $10K a month launch a median **2.8–3 new creatives a week**, with the top quartile at about 4.8. Health & Wellness micro accounts sit at a median of 3 ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/meta-ads-in-2026-how-many-creatives-do-you-actually-need-to-launch/); [Motion by vertical](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/testing-by-vertical)). With a 5–8% hit rate, four new ads a week (2 per lane) yields about **one real winner a month across the account**. That is the right expectation. Meta needs roughly 50 optimization events per ad set per week to exit learning ([adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-learning-phase-50-events-guide)). At $40–85 per trial, $700 a week buys roughly 8–17 trials across both lanes. Trial-optimized ad sets would be permanently Learning Limited. Optimizing on CompleteRegistration is a defensible trade for signal volume. RocketShip's rule of "optimize for trial starts under $1,000/day" assumes trials are frequent enough, and Ripple's are not ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/meta-optimize-trial-starts-vs-paid-conversions-ios/)). The cost of that choice is that Meta will hunt for people who register, not people who pay. So creatives have to be judged on trials and trial-to-paid even though the algorithm optimizes on registration. Splitting a separate testing campaign off this budget would halve the event count, so each lane should run one consolidated ad set.

Kill rules at this budget have to use leading indicators. The common heuristic is to "kill early on attention, late on CPA": cut after 1,000–2,000 impressions if link CTR or hook rate is weak, and make a cost-per-result call only after spending **2–3x the target CPA** ([admanage.ai](https://admanage.ai/blog/creative-testing-budget-guide)). These are generic DTC thresholds, and the 0.5% CTR floor is far below Ripple's 2–4% baseline. Thresholds should be relative to the lane's own median instead. Statistical power is the uncomfortable fact. FunnelFox estimates that detecting a 20% effect at a 1% conversion rate needs about **40,000 users per variant** ([FunnelFox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/creative-testing-for-web2app/)). No creative decision at $100/day is statistically clean. Meta's own spend allocation is the other signal. In a consolidated ad set it concentrates budget on a few ads, and an ad starved for two weeks has in effect been judged ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/andromeda-3/)).

Two account settings affect creative. Burke separates statics and video into different ad sets so that video's pull toward Reels does not starve the Feed statics ([RevenueCat / Burke](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/meta-ads-in-2025-tips-for-apps)). This matters the moment Ripple adds video. Advantage+ creative enhancements are on by default ([AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/facebook-ads/advantage-plus-creative/)). Text-generation and CTA-rewriting enhancements should be off, because an AI-rewritten line like "Feeling overwhelmed?" would break both Meta policy and Ripple's voice.

## Meta policy draws the line at implying the reader's condition or age

The binding rule is **Personal Attributes**. Ads must not assert or imply a person's "physical or mental health (including medical conditions)", age, financial vulnerability and other attributes. Meta's own examples allow "Depression counseling" and prohibit "Depression getting you down?" and "Do you have diabetes?" ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)). Questions count as implication. So "Struggling with anxiety?", "Burned out?" and "Feeling overwhelmed, mom?" are all out. "You" is fine when not tied to an attribute ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-personal-attributes-policy)). Age is an attribute too, so "women over 40 like you" violates the rule. Age belongs in the targeting settings, never in second-person copy. First-person stories ("I didn't realize how much I was carrying until I saw it written down") and plain product descriptions are the safe forms.

The **Health & Wellness** standard bans negative self-perception, sensational or exaggerated health claims, and claims to cure incurable conditions. Since the 22 July 2026 update, Meta reviews claims one by one, and before/after imagery is no longer automatically rejected ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/); [Adligator](https://adligator.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-ad-policy-update-2026)). Ripple's own ban on before/after and outcome claims is stricter than Meta's and should stay that way. A separate risk sits in the plumbing. Since early 2025, advertisers Meta classifies as health-related can lose the ability to optimize for lower-funnel events, and custom conversions that reference health attributes can be disabled ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands); [Polar Analytics](https://www.polaranalytics.com/post/2025-metas-tracking-restrictions-for-health-wellness-are-here----heres-how-to-fix-it)). No primary source confirms whether journaling or habit apps fall into that class. Events Manager should be checked for restriction flags on Ripple's pixel and domain, and event names should stay neutral.

## The weekly pipeline spec

This section is the spec for the weekly AI ad-generation pipeline. It keeps the current shape (10 generated per lane, founder approves 2 per lane, one ad set per lane) and changes what gets generated, how it is written, what gets chosen and how it is judged. Most of the thresholds below are practitioner heuristics adapted to Ripple's baselines, not validated numbers. They should be revised after 6–8 weeks of Ripple's own data.

### Format mix out of 10 per lane

Every batch should cover at least **six distinct format families**, and no two ads in a batch should share both an angle and a format. Seven of the ten should be new concepts. Three should be **iterations of the lane's current best ad**, keeping its winning line and changing the format or visual (or keeping the visual and changing only the opening scene), which is how Liven and Opal extend winners.

| Slot | Ripple lane (women 40–50, Feed-heavy) | BWK lane (men 18–34, Reels-heavy) | Static now? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Demo card**, "you say it → Ripple catches it": a spoken sentence on the left, the resulting to-do list, habit check and pattern on the right | **Demo card**: a spoken line becomes tasks plus a "habit missed 4 days running" flag | Yes |
| 3 | **Pattern / weekly-report reveal** (the winner family): one real-looking insight, e.g. "Same worry, 4 weeks running" | **Scoreboard**: Life Matrix or habit streak shown as a stat card | Yes |
| 4 | **Text wall / Notes-app story**, first person, 5–8 short lines | **Notes-app list**: "things I said I'd do this month" with the misses visible | Yes |
| 5 | **Social-post or letter mockup** ("a note to the me who thought she needed a better planner") | **Identity statement card**, plain type on flat color | Yes |
| 6 | **Real review in native chrome** (App Store, Facebook comment, text message); only verbatim, permissioned quotes | **Real review**, or a stitch-style "reply to a comment" card | Yes |
| 7 | **Infographic**: "what a week of debriefs turns into" (tasks, mood line, top 3 themes, 6-area scores) | **Infographic**: the 6 life areas as a scoreboard over 8 weeks | Yes |
| 8 | **Iteration A** of current best ad: same line, new format | **Iteration A** | Yes |
| 9 | **Iteration B** of current best ad: same format, new opening scene | **Iteration B** | Yes |
| 10 | **Iteration C or offer-first test**: "7-day free trial" in the headline, never price; or a lifestyle photo with baked text, following the visual rules below | **Offer/challenge card** ("30-day run, see your own report") or a photo with baked text | Yes |

App-screenshot cards fold into slots 1, 3 and 7. On their own they are a weak concept; as proof of a claim they are strong. AI photography drops from a default to **at most 2 of 10 per lane**. Motion's hit-rate spread (text-only 11.6% against high production 6.9%) and the NIQ findings both point that way.

### Hook templates in Ripple's voice

Hooks go on the image as the largest text and in the first line of primary text. Every hook must contain a **concrete noun, number or quoted phrase**. Hooks name a situation, never a state or condition of the reader.

| Template | Ripple lane example | BWK lane example |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner-voice confession** (Opal pattern), first person | "I was the only one who knew the dentist was Tuesday." | "I said 'gym tomorrow' four times this week." |
| **Pattern reveal** (Ripple's winner family) | "Same patterns. Different year." / "Three weeks in a row, the same thing came up. I only saw it written down." | "Week 6. Same two habits slipping. Now I can see which ones." |
| **Literal input → output** (Letterly pattern) | "I said: 'renew the car registration, email the school, I'm always the one who remembers.' Ripple heard 2 tasks and 1 pattern." | "Said: 'finish the deck, call Dad, lift, less phone.' Got: 3 tasks, 1 habit missed 4 days running." |
| **The invisible list, made visible** | "The list in my head, finally on paper. All 23 things." | "Every promise I made myself this month, in one list." |
| **Weekly report reveal** | "My week, as Ripple saw it: 14 things handled. 3 slipped. One came up every day." | "Weekly report: focus up, sleep flat, 2 promises kept out of 5." |
| **Identity / standard** (BWK-led) | "Noticing is the first thing I do for myself now." | "Discipline is remembering what you told yourself." |
| **Quiz-as-result** (Rise/Noom pattern; must match quiz screen 1) | "What's taking up the most room in my head? The quiz showed me." | "Take the quiz. See which area of your life you're neglecting." |
| **Contrast with the usual tool** | "My planner never noticed I kept moving the same thing." | "Your notes app doesn't notice when you skip. Ripple does." |

Banned in every lane: questions or statements implying a condition or feeling state ("Overwhelmed?", "Burned out?", "anxious"); any age reference in copy; "brain dump"; any recording duration or fixed time of day for recording; outcome, health or before/after claims; invented numbers of users, stars or press.

### Copy structure rules

Primary text has four parts in fixed order. **First**, the hook or scene, which must fit in the first 125 characters. **Second**, one literal mechanism line built from `PRODUCT_WHAT_IT_DOES`, for example "You talk to Ripple any time. It turns what you said into your to-do list, tracks your habits and mood, and shows what keeps coming up." **Third**, one proof line, only if it is true (a verbatim review, or a product fact such as "scores 6 areas of your life and sends a weekly report"). **Fourth**, a soft bridge to the quiz ("Take the quiz to see what you're carrying"). The headline is outcome-led, 3–7 words ("See what's really going on", "Your week, finally on paper", "Keep the promises you make"). The CTA is always "Learn more" to the web quiz. Each ad ships with a short (≤125 characters) and a long (≤600 characters) human-written primary-text variant. Price never appears in the ad. "7-day free trial" appears only in the designated offer-test slot until data says otherwise. **The first quiz screen must repeat the ad's promise.** An ad about the invisible list should land on a question about the invisible list, because a mismatch between ad and product is the most-cited cause of trial leakage.

### Visual direction and AI-image fatigue

The default look is **phone-native and plain**: Notes, iMessage, App Store review, Facebook post, a lined notebook, flat color with large type, real Ripple UI rendered crisply. Motion's text-only and "ugly ad" data and Burke's text-wall recommendation all support this. Ripple's recent winners are already of this type. When the pipeline does generate photos, AI becomes the **setting, never the subject**. Use objects and places (a kitchen counter with a phone and car keys, a car dashboard, a desk with a half-crossed list, a gym bench), with no front-facing synthetic faces, no glossy stock-mood lighting, and no "woman gazing out a window". Each batch should rotate a **visual system**: typographic, UI-led, native-mockup and photographic, so style variety follows the concept variety instead of replacing it. The Opus image check should add one rejection criterion: "would a viewer recognize this as AI in under a second?" Any image that fails gets regenerated as a typographic card instead. A 4:5 version goes to Feed. A 9:16 version with text inside the safe zone is produced for every ad, so Reels delivery is not wasted on a cropped square.

### What the founder should pick

The two weekly approvals per lane follow a rubric, not taste. **Pick 1 is the strongest new concept**: the batch's best specific, insight-or-demo ad, with a concrete noun or number in the hook, the literal mechanism visible within a second, and no policy risk. **Pick 2 must differ from pick 1 in both angle and format family.** In alternate weeks, it is a new concept from a family the lane has not tested in the last three weeks. In the other weeks, it is an iteration of the lane's current best ad, to extend a proven line. Reject any ad whose hook could appear in a competitor's ad unchanged ("Find your calm"), any ad whose image outshines its words, and any ad that repeats the angle of an ad already running. Log each approved ad with tags for **angle, format family, visual system, hook template and lane**. The unit of learning is the concept, so these tags are what the weekly generation prompt should read back.

### Testing, kill and scale rules at ~$100/day

Each lane runs one consolidated ad set optimizing CompleteRegistration, with Advantage+ text enhancements off. New ads launch into that ad set. Set a lane's target cost per trial at the current median, about $50–60. The thresholds are these.

| Checkpoint | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ~1,500 impressions or ~$20 spend | Link CTR below **half the lane's trailing median** (e.g. <1.0–1.5%), or cost per landing-page view >2x median | Turn off; tag the concept "weak attention" |
| ~$50–70 spend | Quiz first-answer rate from this ad (utm_content) under half the lane median | Turn off; tag "ad-to-funnel mismatch" |
| 2x target trial cost (~$100–120) with **zero trials** | No trial | Turn off (automated rule) |
| 14 days live, under 5% of ad set spend | Meta starved it | Turn off; do not duplicate to force delivery |
| ≥3x target trial cost spent | Cost per trial ≤ target, and trial-to-paid not below lane average | Declare a **winner**; queue 2–3 iterations |
| Winner, trailing 14 days | CTR down ≥30% from its first two weeks, or frequency >3.5 | Refresh with a new visual or opening scene under the same line; keep the original running while cost per trial holds |
| Winner, trailing 14 days | Cost per trial >1.5x target | Retire. "Winners are never paused" should mean "never paused while they still perform" |

Keep **6–8 active ads per lane** at most. Beyond that, new ads rarely get the delivery they need to be read. Scale budget in steps of 15–20% no more often than every 3–4 days, and only while the lane's blended cost per trial and trial-to-paid hold. Once video exists, run it in its own ad set per lane so Reels delivery does not starve the Feed statics.

### What to measure

The minimum per-ad record is: spend, share of ad-set spend, impressions, frequency, link CTR, cost per landing-page view, click-to-LPV ratio, and quiz first-answer rate. After those: email or registration rate, CompleteRegistration cost, trial starts, cost per trial, and **trial-to-paid by ad, placement and age band**, joined through utm_content into the Stripe customer metadata. Report placement splits (Facebook Feed, Facebook Reels, Instagram Feed, Instagram Reels) weekly for each lane, because a lane whose trials drift to Instagram Reels will show good trial costs and weak paid conversion. Add a one-question "where did you hear about us" survey after trial start to cross-check Meta attribution, as Burke recommends at this spend level. The weekly generation prompt should receive a table of **concept tags against these metrics**, not just a list of winning images, so it learns which angles work rather than which colors do. Before choosing optimization events, confirm the pixel and domain carry no health-and-wellness restriction flag in Events Manager.

### Static now versus video later

Everything in the format mix above can ship in the current static pipeline this week. The recommendations that need video are these. First, **screen-recording demos** of a real debrief turning into tasks, which Motion ranks among the top formats and which is the most direct test of Ripple's mechanism. Second, **faceless AI voiceover over B-roll**, which Burke names as the rising cheap app format. Third, **tutorial or review UGC** from real users, which beat testimonials in AppsFlyer's data. Fourth, **9:16 Reels-native creative** with audio, where Meta's tests show a 34.5% cost advantage. Fifth, **stitch, reaction and POV** formats, which lead the Health & Wellness hit-rate ranking. The cheapest bridge is the **static-to-video hybrid**: animating the demo card (the spoken line typing in, the list appearing) through the existing Higgsfield/Kling setup. Motion lists this format among its high hit-rate formats, and it reuses statics that have already won. Video should start in the BWK lane, because young men sit on Reels and statics under-deliver there. The Ripple lane can stay static-led longer, because its audience is on Facebook Feed.

## Conclusion

The evidence shifts the question from "which format" to "which concept, stated how plainly". Category leaders do not win by making better-looking ads. They find one line that makes the product's value obvious and keep it running for a year under changing visuals. Ripple has probably already found two or three such lines. The biggest near-term gain is to stop spending weekly slots on unrelated new moods and start treating "Same patterns. Different year." as a line to extend across formats, lanes and eventually video. The second is the open competitive gap. No one else is showing overloaded midlife women their own words turned into a list and a pattern, so the demo and pattern-reveal families are Ripple's own territory, not something borrowed from a template.

The hardest constraint is statistical, not creative. At $100 a day, Ripple will get about one real winner a month, and most per-ad calls will rest on click and quiz signals rather than trials. That makes two cheap disciplines worth more than any format change: tagging every ad by concept so the pipeline learns across weeks, and joining ad IDs to trial-to-paid in Stripe so that a cheap-trial ad drawing the wrong people is caught before it is scaled.
